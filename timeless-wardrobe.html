Building a timeless wardrobe

The tailored classics that keep returning, how to wear them now, and how to buy fewer, better pieces that last.

Essential one

The trench coat

The trench coat is perhaps the most enduring outerwear design of the last century. Look for a double-breasted front, a storm flap, a belt and a length that suits your height, usually just above or at the knee.

  • Classic khaki or stone works with almost everything.
  • Navy and black feel more urban and formal.
  • Wear it open over knitwear, or belted for structure.
Essential two

The tailored blazer

A well-cut blazer instantly sharpens jeans, dresses or trousers. Gabardine and wool twill blazers keep their shape and travel well.

Choose a soft shoulder for relaxed outfits and a structured shoulder for formal ones. Navy, charcoal and camel are the most versatile.

Essential three

Trousers that hang beautifully

Gabardine trousers are the quiet hero of a timeless wardrobe. A pressed crease elongates the leg, and the smooth cloth falls cleanly from hip to hem.

  • Straight leg: the most versatile, with loafers or boots.
  • Wide leg: elegant and relaxed; hem to just above the floor with your shoes on.
  • Pleated: extra room and movement, a nod to mid-century style.

Cost per wear

A well-made piece worn weekly for years often costs less per wear than several cheap ones.

Alter, don’t abandon

Small tailoring changes can make a near-fit perfect and bring old favourites back to life.

Neutral base

Build around a few neutrals, then add colour with knits and accessories.

The echo

How classics return

Fashion moves in cycles. Wide trousers, strong shoulders and long belted coats have all come back more than once. Each return borrows the original idea and changes the proportions, colours or fabrics.

Buying classics with good fabric and fit means your wardrobe is always ready for the next echo.

Storage

Keep tailoring in shape

  • Use broad, shaped hangers for coats and blazers.
  • Empty pockets so they don’t sag.
  • Give garments space in the wardrobe so they don’t crush.
  • Store off-season wool clean, in breathable covers, with cedar.
