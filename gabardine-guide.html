The gabardine guide

Where gabardine came from, how it’s woven, the fibres it’s made from, and how to choose and care for gabardine garments.

History

From weatherproof cloth to wardrobe icon

Gabardine was developed in Britain in the late 1870s as a breathable, weather-resistant fabric for outdoor clothing. The yarn was treated before weaving and then woven very tightly, creating a cloth that shed wind and rain far better than traditional heavy rubberised fabrics while still letting the body breathe.

It soon clothed explorers, aviators and soldiers, and its most famous form, the belted trench coat, moved from military life into civilian wardrobes after the First World War.

Fibres

Wool, cotton and blends

Wool gabardine drapes beautifully, resists creasing and breathes well, making it a favourite for trousers and suits that need to look sharp all day. Cotton gabardine is crisp and hard-wearing, ideal for trench coats and casual trousers.

Blends with polyester or other fibres add durability and easy care, while a little elastane adds comfort stretch. Check the label to know how a garment will feel and how to look after it.

Touch

Good gabardine feels smooth and firm, never papery. Run a hand diagonally to feel the fine ribs.

Look

The twill lines should be fine, steep and even across the whole garment.

Drape

Hold it up: wool gabardine falls in clean folds; cotton feels crisper and more structured.

Tailoring

Why tailors love gabardine

Gabardine holds a sharp crease and a clean line, so trousers keep their press and coats keep their shape. Its density means seams sit flat and buttonholes stay neat.

The trade-off: the smooth surface can turn shiny if pressed directly with a hot iron. Tailors always use a pressing cloth and steam.

Care

Caring for gabardine

  • Brush wool gabardine after wear to lift dust from the weave.
  • Hang trousers by the hem or on a clamp hanger to keep the crease.
  • Rest garments for a day between wears so fibres recover.
  • Press with steam through a damp cloth; never iron directly.
  • Dry clean wool sparingly; wash cotton versions gently if the label allows.
Weather

How weather-resistant is it?

Gabardine’s tight weave resists wind and light rain, and many garments receive a water-repellent finish. It is still a breathable cloth rather than a waterproof membrane, so in sustained heavy rain it will eventually wet through.

Refresh water repellency as the maker recommends, and let damp garments dry naturally on a wide hanger, away from direct heat.
