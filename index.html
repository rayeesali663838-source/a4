    Measure like a tailor

    Five measurements worth knowing

    1. Chest: around the fullest part, under the arms, tape level.

    2. Shoulder: across the back from one shoulder point to the other.

    3. Sleeve: from shoulder point to the wrist bone, arm slightly bent.

    4. Waist: where you normally wear your trousers, not where you think you should.

    5. Inseam: from crotch to the floor, wearing the shoes you’ll pair with the trousers.

    Colour and cloth

    Why khaki became a classic

    Gabardine was first woven in the late nineteenth century as a weatherproof cloth for outdoor wear. Its earthy khaki and sand tones were practical, hiding dust and mud, and they have remained the defining colour of the trench coat ever since.

    Today the same neutral palette anchors a timeless wardrobe: khaki and stone with navy, white and black, warmed up with camel and rust in autumn.

    Ask the editors
    What is gabardine?

    Gabardine is a tightly woven twill fabric with a smooth face and fine diagonal ribs that run at a steep angle. The dense weave makes it durable, wind resistant and naturally water-repellent, which is why it is used for trench coats, trousers and suits.

    Is gabardine wool or cotton?

    It can be either, or a blend. Wool gabardine is popular for suits and trousers because it drapes well and resists creasing. Cotton gabardine is common for trench coats and chinos. Synthetic and blended versions also exist.

    Is gabardine waterproof?

    Gabardine is water-resistant rather than fully waterproof. Its dense weave sheds light rain and wind, and it is often treated with a water-repellent finish. In heavy rain, water will eventually soak through.

    How do I care for gabardine?

    Follow the care label. Wool gabardine usually needs dry cleaning and regular brushing; cotton gabardine can often be washed gently. Press with a cloth between iron and fabric to avoid shine.

    What is the difference between gabardine and twill?

    Gabardine is a type of twill. All twills have diagonal ribs, but gabardine has a much tighter weave and a steeper rib, giving it a smoother, denser surface than everyday twills like chino or drill.
